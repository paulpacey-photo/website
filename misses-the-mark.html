Why Your Marketing Photos Are Missing the Mark

You don’t need to be a photographer to judge a great photo. You need four simple questions, and a quick test of your eye at the end.

Before a parent reads a word,
your imagery has already spoken.

Your school only gets one first impression. Long before prospective parents read about your curriculum or values, your imagery has already created an instant emotional connection, or a subtle reason to move on. Knowing which is easier than you think.

You don’t need to be a photographer to judge a photograph objectively.
You need four simple questions.

Four simple questions
  1. 01Does it look good?Aesthetic
  2. 02Does it feel good?Emotion
  3. 03Does it tell a story?Narrative
  4. 04Is it believable?Authenticity
Aesthetic
01

Does it look good?

The foundation, not the finish. A technically solid image isn’t enough on its own, but every other layer is built on it. It’s also where a surprising number of school images already fall short.

  • Exposure: Is it too dark or too light? Can you clearly see the faces?
  • Composition: Does it feel balanced? Is anything awkwardly cut off? Is there more in the frame than it needs?
  • Focus: Is it obvious what the image is about? Is that subject sharp when you zoom in?
  • Light: Is the light flattering to faces, or is it either lifeless and flat or too harsh with heavy shadows?
  • Color: Do skin tones look natural and healthy?
Emotion
02

Does it feel good?

An emotionally charged photo—even one with technical flaws—will always beat a technically perfect image that feels empty. Capturing genuine emotion is the hardest part of visual storytelling, but when combined with true authenticity, it becomes the single most powerful driver of trust and connection.

  • Feeling: Does it express a positive emotion or state of mind, such as joy, curiosity, focus, pride or belonging?
  • Expressions: Are they genuine, or performed for the camera?
  • Connection: Is there a bond between people, or between a student and their work?
  • Relatability: Could a parent picture their own child in this moment?
  • Impact: Does it make you feel something?
Narrative
03

Does it tell a story?

Narrative is the context behind your message: who’s there, what’s happening, and why it matters.

  • Moment: Is there a defining moment that tells a bigger story?
  • Clarity: Is it understood at a glance, or does it need explaining?
Exception to the ruleSometimes, less is more. For hero imagery—like poster or billboard shots—a busy narrative can actually distract. A clean, minimal focus on a single student or teacher creates a stronger visual impact and leaves room for headline copy and graphics to deliver the message.
Authenticity · The final test
04

Is it believable?

An image can look good, feel good, tell a story—and still fail completely if it isn’t believable. Instead of building trust, a clearly manufactured image signals that the reality of your school doesn’t match the marketing.

  • Unscripted: Does the moment look and feel natural, or does everything look a little too rigid or orchestrated?
  • Engagement: Are people engaged with each other or their work, rather than posing for the camera?
  • Spontaneous: Bonus Is there an unmistakable sense of an unplanned moment or interaction?
