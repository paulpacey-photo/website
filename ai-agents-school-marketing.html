Eight New Hires Your Marketing Team Doesn’t Need to Interview For

Three areas where AI agents belong in school marketing.

Paul PaceyPhotographer to international schools · Prague

If I ran marketing at an international school today, I’d set up eight AI agents, organized around the three moments that decide enrollment: being found, being chosen, and keeping families.

For 15 years I’ve worked inside school marketing campaigns, across more than 200 shoots in over 30 countries. The pattern I see everywhere isn’t a lack of talent or ideas. It’s a lack of time. Small marketing teams carry admissions events, newsletters, social media, the website, agency briefs and the head’s latest request, all at once. The work that would make the biggest difference is usually the work nobody has time for.

That’s exactly where AI agents earn their place. An agent is simply an AI assistant given one standing job: it watches, gathers, drafts and reminds, so your team can spend its time on the things only people can do. None of the eight below replaces anyone. Each one gives a busy team back a few hours a week, and between them they close the gaps where families are quietly lost.

Being found

Before a family can choose you, they have to find you, and the way they search is changing fast.

1. The search and AI visibility agent

More and more parents now ask an AI assistant “What’s the best international school in Zurich for a 10-year-old?” instead of typing a search into Google. The answer they get depends on what the AI can find and trust about your school.

This agent checks how your school appears in Google results and in AI answers. It makes sure your key facts (curriculum, ages, fees, location, accreditation) are consistent across your website and the school directories parents use. It flags pages that search engines can’t read well, and suggests the questions your website should answer but doesn’t.

Why it matters: a school that doesn’t show up in the answer never makes the shortlist, however good it is.

2. The competitor and market watch agent

Most marketing teams know their direct competitors. Few have time to watch them closely.

This agent keeps an eye on the schools you compete with: new campuses, fee changes, new programs, campaigns, and how their messaging is shifting. It also watches the market around you, such as a multinational announcing a new regional office, or an embassy expanding. Those are families arriving next year. Once a month it sends a short briefing: what changed, and what it might mean for you.

Why it matters: positioning only works relative to the schools beside you. If everyone claims the same things, you need to know before you say them too.

Being chosen

Once a family has found you, the decision usually comes down to two things: how they’re treated, and whether they believe what they see.

3. The enquiry follow-up agent

Most schools already put real people and real care into this stage, and rightly so. A relocating family often enquires at three or four schools in the same week, and the school that replies quickly, personally and warmly starts with a real advantage. The challenge is rarely effort. It’s timing: enquiries peak at weekends, during open days and from families in other time zones, exactly when even a well-staffed team can’t reply at once.

This agent doesn’t replace your admissions team; it makes sure no family waits for them. It drafts a personal reply to every enquiry within the hour, in the family’s own language, answering their actual questions and suggesting tour dates. It keeps track of families who have gone quiet, with a gentle follow-up ready. A member of the admissions team reads and sends every message, so the personal touch your team already gives stays exactly where it is.

Why it matters: every family lost at this stage cost the whole marketing budget to reach.

4. The admissions funnel analyst

Admissions teams track a great deal, but the information usually lives in several places at once: the enquiry form, the admissions system, spreadsheets, email, and the notes in people’s heads. Pulling it together into one clear picture takes hours few teams have.

This agent does that work continuously. It follows each stage of the journey, from enquiry to tour, application, offer and enrollment, and shows where families drop out, which sources bring the families who actually enroll, and how this year compares with last. It turns “we need more enquiries” into a sharper question, such as “we’re losing families between the tour and the application, so why?”

Why it matters: you can’t fix what you can’t see, and the cheapest new families are often the ones you’re already losing.

5. The story finder

This is the agent closest to my own work. Every school produces remarkable stories every single day: a science project that went somewhere unexpected, a student who found her voice in drama, a teacher who turned a field trip into a lesson nobody will forget. Most never reach the marketing team.

This agent gathers them from where they already live: newsletters, teacher posts, the school calendar, class blogs. It flags the moments worth telling, and builds a content calendar from them. It also knows what’s coming up, so the moments worth capturing on camera are planned for, not discovered afterwards.

Why it matters: parents don’t choose a school because of its claims. They choose it because of the moments that make those claims believable.

6. The content repurposing agent

A good story deserves more than one outing. This agent takes one strong story and drafts the versions each channel needs: a newsletter item, two or three social posts, a website update, a line for the admissions email. Everything is written in your school’s voice, and your team edits before anything goes out.

Why it matters: it multiplies the value of every story, without multiplying the team’s workload.

Keeping families

Enrollment doesn’t end on the first day of school. Your current families are your most trusted marketing channel, and the families you keep never have to be won again.

7. The reviews and reputation agent

What parents say about you online now carries as much weight as anything you say yourself. This agent watches your Google reviews, parent forums and expat community groups. It drafts thoughtful replies to reviews for your team to approve, and flags any concern early, before a single unhappy post becomes a thread everyone reads.

Why it matters: relocating families trust other parents more than any brochure. You want to know what they’re hearing.

8. The retention signals agent

Families rarely leave without warning. The signs are usually there: lower scores in a parent survey, a re-enrollment form that hasn’t come back, a sibling’s application that quietly stalls. The trouble is that these signals sit in different places, seen by different people.

This agent brings them together and gives your team a short list of families who may need a personal conversation. What happens next stays human: a call from the right teacher or the head, at the right time.

Why it matters: keeping a family costs far less than finding a new one, and a family that stays becomes your best advocate.

Three lines I wouldn’t cross

Agents make a small team far more capable. They also make it easier to cut corners that matter. Schools hold something more precious than most businesses do: the trust of families with their children. So I’d hold three firm lines.

  • Keep children’s data out of it. Student names, photos, records and anything that identifies a child stay out of AI tools unless your data protection set-up is watertight, and your data protection lead has signed it off. Most of these agents don’t need student data at all.
  • A person sends every message to a family. Agents draft; people decide. One clumsy automated email, with the wrong name or the wrong tone at the wrong moment, can undo years of goodwill.
  • No AI-generated “students.” It’s tempting to fill a gap in your image library with a generated picture. Don’t. Parents are choosing a real community, and the moment they suspect your pictures aren’t real, they start to wonder what else isn’t. In an age of AI imagery, authenticity is your strongest asset.

Where to start

Don’t build all eight at once. Start where your team is stretched thinnest, prove it works, then add the next.

In my experience, admissions is usually the best-resourced part of a school’s marketing effort. The gaps tend to sit around it: the stories that never get told, the AI answers nobody is checking, and the families who quietly drift away. That’s where I’d begin:

  • 1. The story finder. It feeds every channel you have, and every campaign you’ll run.
  • 2. Search and AI visibility. It decides whether families find you at all, and almost nobody is watching it yet.
  • 3. Retention signals. It protects the families you’ve already won.

The rest can follow as your team gets comfortable working alongside them.

Technology won’t make a school worth choosing. That part is already there, in your classrooms, every day. What agents can do is give your team the time to find it, tell it well and make sure the right families see it. You don’t need to manufacture a story. Just hold up a mirror to the magic that’s already there.
